There are 41 different F2L situations. You do not need 41 sequences. Most of them are a **short setup** away from a situation you already know. The method is always the same:

**awkward situation → setup → familiar situation → insertion**

## A corner stuck in its slot

The hardest-looking pairs have a piece sitting in the slot the wrong way. Here the corner is in its own slot with its white sticker turned, and its edge is on top. You cannot insert anything: the slot is not empty.

<Cube setup="R U' R' U R U' R' U" alg="U' R U R' U' R U R'" controls="true" highlight="FDR RDF DFR UF FU" label="A corner stuck in its slot, edge on top. The first four turns lift the corner out, one turn lines it up, and the last three insert the pair." />

Watch it in three parts.

1. **U' R U R' lifts the corner out.** U' brings the edge round to the right. R opens the slot and lifts the corner out of it (the edge goes round the back with it). U moves the corner off to the side, and R' closes the slot again. The corner is now in the top layer and the slot is empty.
2. **U' lines the pieces up.** The corner's white sticker faces the side and the edge is behind it.
3. **R U R' inserts.** This is the exact situation from the last lesson.

So a stuck corner is not a new case. It is the case you know, after **getting the corner out and lining it up**.

## Why the cross survives

Every turn that opens the slot is undone before the sequence ends: R is closed by R'. The turns in between only move pieces in the top layer, which the cross and the finished slots do not care about. When you make your own setup, check the same thing: whatever you open, close.

## Your turn

Below are three cubes. Each has one lit pair, a solved cross and the other three slots finished. You turn the cube with the buttons (or the keys U, R, L and F, with Shift for the opposite direction). It checks the cube after every turn, so **any** solution that puts the lit pair home and leaves the cross and the other slots alone counts, not just the one shown afterwards.

If you are stuck, ask for a hint. They get stronger one at a time: what to look at, then what to aim for, then a first turn. You can also ask to see a solution.

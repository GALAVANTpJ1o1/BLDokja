Two-look OLL turns the top face yellow in **two looks**. First you make the four **edges** yellow on top. That gives you a yellow cross. Then you make the four **corners** yellow on top. Each look is a small picture you can name.

## Look one: the edges

Ignore the corners completely. Only the yellow centre and the four top edges matter, so the pictures below show only those. There are only three patterns.

- **Dot:** no edge is yellow on top.
- **Line:** two opposite edges are yellow, forming a line across the top.
- **L:** two neighbouring edges are yellow, forming an L.

Each card is drawn the way its algorithm expects, with yellow on top. If your cube shows the same pattern turned round, turn the top layer (U) until it matches. Play the algorithm on the cube to see it turn the pattern into a cross.

<CaseCards stage="eo" />

Notice that the Dot algorithm is simply the Line algorithm followed by the L algorithm. Two small algorithms you know make the third.

## Look two: the corners

Now the four edges are yellow, so you have a yellow cross, and only the corners need turning. Count how many corners already have yellow on top, then look at where the other yellow stickers are pointing. There are seven pictures. Two of them have names you will hear all the time: **Sune** and **Anti-Sune**. They are mirror images of one another and come up most often.

<CaseCards stage="co" />

For all seven, the four edges stay yellow on top. The pieces may still end up in the wrong places, and that is for the next lesson.

## Practise recognising before executing

Recognition and execution are two different skills. In the recognition practice you see a case, name it, and only then watch its algorithm play. It runs the whole thing in order, edges then corners, on one cube: what the first algorithm leaves behind is what you see next.

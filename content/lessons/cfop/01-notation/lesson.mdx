Already solve a 3×3 with the beginner method? This independent CFOP path starts with notation, then F2L. It does not teach the beginner method or require blindfolded lessons. Cubes on this path are drawn with **white on the bottom (your cross) and yellow on top (the last layer)**.

## Read one turn

The face names describe where a face is **now**, not its colour: U is up, D down, F front, B back, R right and L left. A bare letter is a clockwise quarter turn, viewed straight at that face. An apostrophe reverses it; a 2 means a half turn.

<MoveExplorer moves="R R' R2 L L' U U' U2 D F B" label="CFOP face turns: choose a move and inspect its effect" />

Try R, then R'. They undo each other. Try R2 twice. **Previous move** removes just your latest press; **Start over** returns this explorer to solved. Each explorer has its own history.

Do not judge B clockwise while looking at F. Imagine walking round to the back and facing B first. The same rule applies to L and D.

## Two layers are not the whole cube

A lowercase **r** turns the right outer layer and its neighbouring middle layer together. It is also written Rw, and the two mean exactly the same. Lowercase l, f, u, d and b work the same way, and the algorithms on this site use them. M turns only the middle layer between L and R, in L's direction.

x, y and z rotate the entire cube in the directions of R, U and F respectively. A rotation changes your viewpoint, not whether the cube is solved. After y, the face now in front is F, even though its colour has changed.

<MoveExplorer moves="r r' M M' x x' y y' z z'" label="CFOP wide moves, slices and rotations" />

## Learn sequences in small chunks

Read one move, make it, then check your hands against the cube. Use the net or text view for exact sticker positions; **Inspect in 3D** adds a spatial view. On a physical cube, start slowly enough to notice an accidental extra U turn. Speed comes after repeatable execution.

You do not need to learn an algorithm list before starting F2L. First learn what a pair is, where it belongs and how opening a slot lets it enter.

The last layer has two separate jobs, and mixing them up is the commonest reason a case looks confusing.

**Orientation** asks: is the sticker on top **yellow**? A piece can be in the wrong place and still be oriented. **Permutation** asks: is each piece in the **right place**? A piece can be in the right place and still be twisted the wrong way.

The order matters. Get every top sticker yellow first (OLL, for orientation of the last layer). Only then look at where the pieces are (PLL, for permutation of the last layer). Once the top is all yellow, the top face tells you nothing more, so the side colours are what you read.

## Two looks or one

Learning all 57 orientation cases and all 21 permutation cases is a big job, so there is a smaller route: **two-look**.

- **Two-look OLL** orients the edges first (3 pictures), then the corners (7 pictures).
- **Two-look PLL** permutes the corners first (2 pictures), then the edges (4 pictures).

That is 16 pictures instead of 78, and it finishes the same cube. Full OLL and full PLL do each stage in one algorithm and are faster, but you can add them one case at a time whenever you like.

## Hold the case, then turn

Every case card on this site shows the case exactly as its algorithm expects, with yellow on top. If your cube looks like the card turned sideways, turn the top layer (a **U** turn) until it matches, or hold the cube so it does. The cards say which. When an algorithm needs a turn before or after it, that is written on the card too.

Full PLL fixes corners and edges together in **one algorithm**. There are **21 cases**. Because the top is already yellow, the whole case lives on the **ring** of side stickers around the top layer.

## Read the ring

Start with what stands out.

- **A bar**: one side shows three matching stickers.
- **Headlights**: one side shows matching corners with a different colour between them.
- **Neither**: no side matches. Then read how the colours run from corner to corner round the ring.

The cards below are drawn with the top face quiet and the ring loud. A solid outline marks a side with a bar, a dashed outline a side with headlights, and each sticker carries a colour letter, so you never rely on colour alone. If the case is turned, a U turn brings the bar or the headlights to the position the card shows.

## Mirror pairs

Many PLL cases come as a **pair**: the same picture and its reflection. Ja and Jb, Ra and Rb, Aa and Ab are examples. They are easy to confuse, and confusing them means using the wrong algorithm. Compare them side by side, and find the one feature that differs: which side the bar or the headlights are on.

## In the order that saves time

You already have two-look PLL. Add full cases where they save a look, and keep two-look for the rest.

- Learn the cases that come up most, and the ones that are quick to execute.
- Group by what moves: **edges only** (H, Ua, Ub, Z), **adjacent corner swaps**, and **diagonal corner swaps**. Cases in one group share features, so learning one makes the next easier.
- Every algorithm may need a **U turn before** to bring the case to its picture, and one **after** to line the layer up with the centres. The card says which.

A standard 3×3 has no separate PLL parity: if the last layer looks as though one pair is swapped, check the earlier solve or the assembly. Do not import a fix from a bigger cube.

Full OLL does in **one algorithm** what two-look OLL does in two. There are **57 cases**, because the top can show any combination of oriented edges and corners. You are not expected to learn them all at once, and you do not have to: two-look OLL always works, and every full OLL case you add simply saves a look.

## Read the shape

You read a full case as a whole picture: which stickers on top are yellow, **and** where the yellow stickers on the sides point. Unlike two-look, the corners are part of the picture, so nothing is hidden. The cases fall into **families** by the shape the yellow makes: dot, cross, square, lightning bolts, fish, knight move, P, T, C, W, I, small L, awkward shapes, and the two with all four corners already oriented. Learn a family at a time and its members start to look alike.

Each case has a number (OLL 27), and some have a name you will hear at any competition. The seven cross cases below are the ones behind the two-look corner cases you already know. **OLL 27 is Sune and OLL 26 is Anti-Sune.** So Sune is a full OLL case you already know.

## Adding cases without losing two-look

The safest way to grow is to keep two-look as your fallback, then replace it one case at a time.

- Start with the families that come up most, and the cases that need only a few turns.
- Learn a case by its **picture first**. If you can name the shape, you can look the algorithm up.
- When a two-look finish is slower than a full algorithm you know, use the full one. Otherwise keep the two-look.

The full reference shows all 57, grouped by family. You do not read it top to bottom; you go straight to the family you are learning, or search by number or name.

## Hold it, then turn it

Every algorithm is written for the case **as drawn**, yellow on top. If the top layer is a quarter turn away, a single U turn brings it round, and the card says so where one is needed. When an algorithm uses rotations (x, y, z) or wide turns (r, l, f, u), they are part of the notation and it will say how to hold the cube again afterwards.

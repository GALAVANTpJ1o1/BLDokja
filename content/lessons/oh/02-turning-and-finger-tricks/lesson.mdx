Start with U turns. Many right-handed solvers push U with the index and pull U' with the index or middle finger; left-handed solvers can mirror the idea. The exact finger is less important than ending in a stable grip. R and R' normally come from the ring, middle or wrist-assisted movement; practise them slowly enough to keep the cube square.

<MoveExplorer moves="U U' U2 R R' R2 F F' F2 x y" label="One-handed finger-trick practice moves" />

A **regrip** deliberately changes where the fingers sit. A **rotation** changes the whole cube’s viewpoint. Both can be useful, but neither is free: count them while practising an algorithm. If two sequences solve the same case, the better OH choice is usually the one you can repeat with fewer awkward resets on your own hardware.

<Cube alg="R U R' U R U2 R'" controls="true" label="A right-side OLL trigger for studying one-handed regrips" />

The sequence above is the Sune, an orientation algorithm that is the same in both styles. Some other algorithms are not: the site has dedicated one-handed versions of many last-layer cases, and the next lessons show them. Whichever you use, record what your own hand can execute cleanly.

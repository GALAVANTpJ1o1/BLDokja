One-handed solving uses the same notation and CFOP stages as two-handed solving. The change is mechanical: one hand holds, regrips and turns. Start with the thumb resting lightly on F or R, the ring and little fingers supporting the back, and the index and middle fingers free for U turns. Use the hand that is comfortable; the goal is stability, not copying a particular competitor’s grip.

<MoveExplorer moves="U U' U2 R R' R2 F F'" label="One-handed fundamentals: practise controlled turns" />

Keep pressure low. If a turn locks, stop, square the layers and reset the grip rather than forcing through it. A cube that is slightly too loose or overly magnetic changes which finger tricks work, so tune your practice cube before judging an algorithm.

<Cube alg="R U R' U'" controls="true" label="One-handed right-side trigger shown with replay controls" />

This is standard notation, not an OH-only algorithm. In OH, its appeal is that the hand can keep one stable grip while the index manages U and the fingers manage R. Pause and step it before trying it on a physical cube.

A piece can be in exactly the right place and still be wrong: a corner turned a third of a turn, or an edge flipped over. Tracing never walks into it, because nothing points there. So it needs its own treatment.

## Spotting one

After you've traced and broken through every loop, look for pieces that are home but turned:

- A **twisted corner** shows its top or bottom colour on a side face instead.
- A **flipped edge** has both colours on the wrong faces.

A piece like that isn't solved, so it counts when you look for somewhere to break in.

## Tracing it as two targets

With Old Pochmann you handle it with the tools you already have: you trace it as a tiny cycle of **two targets**.

1. **Break in** on one of its stickers, following the usual rule: the lowest letter.
2. **Read** what's sitting in that sticker's place. It's another sticker of the same piece, because the piece is home. Write that letter down, and the little cycle is closed.

Shooting those two targets moves the piece out and back in, turned the right way. It costs two targets, which is why faster methods handle twists differently later on.

## When the buffer is the twisted one

If the buffer's own piece is home but turned, you don't write anything for it. Every other piece gets its targets as usual; once they're all solved, the buffer can only be solved too, because the cube can't have just one corner twisted or just one edge flipped.

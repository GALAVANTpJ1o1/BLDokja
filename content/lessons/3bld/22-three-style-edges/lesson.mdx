Edges work the same way as corners: every pair in the edge memo is one commutator that cycles the buffer and two targets. What's new is the moves the comms are built from.

## The buffer

This site's 3-style edges use the buffer **UF**. From UF there are **440** edge cases, each with a verified commutator.

## Slices do a lot of the work

Edge comms often use the middle slices, **M**, **S** and **E**, as the interchange or inside the insertion, because a slice turn moves edges between faces without touching any corner.

## Flipped edges

An edge in its slot but flipped isn't a 3-cycle, so it isn't a commutator case. Like twisted corners, flipped edges have their own verified algs; learn them after the cycles.

## Where to start

The same route as corners works: pure comms first, then cases by setup, and each case together with its inverse. If you already use M2, cases on the M slice will look familiar.

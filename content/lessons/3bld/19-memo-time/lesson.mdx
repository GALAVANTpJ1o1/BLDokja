Once your solves are coming out, memo is usually most of your time. It has three parts, and they get faster in different ways.

## Three parts of memo

1. **Tracing:** finding each target on the cube.
2. **Encoding:** turning each letter pair into its image and placing it.
3. **Review:** going over the memo once before the blindfold goes on.

A slow memo is almost always slow in one of them. Find out which before changing anything.

## Measure it

The guided trace trainer times every target. The Progress page splits those times by kind: the first target, normal targets, cycle breaks, and twists or flips. If breaks take twice as long as normal targets, that's your practice for the week, not memorising faster.

For encoding, the letter-pair trainer's rapid-fire drill times each pair. A pair that takes seconds to recall its image slows every memo it appears in; replace or re-learn that image.

## Practise the parts separately

- **Tracing only.** Trace scrambles and type the letters, without memorising anything. Aim for no hesitation at breaks.
- **Encoding only.** Drill pairs until an image comes before you've finished reading the letters.
- **Put it together** only when both are quick. Speed you haven't built in the parts won't appear in a full solve.

## Keep accuracy

Speed that costs accuracy isn't worth it. A useful rule while you're cutting time: push speed until one solve in five fails, then hold that pace until it stops failing. And keep looking at every failed cube, the way the last lesson showed.

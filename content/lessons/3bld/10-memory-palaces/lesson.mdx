Images are easier to remember than letters, but a long string of images can still blur or swap order. A **memory palace** fixes that: every image gets a place, and the places already have an order you'll never forget.

## Pick a route

Choose somewhere you know without thinking: your home, a walk to school or work, a game level you've played a hundred times. Then choose a fixed sequence of spots along it: the front door, the doormat, the coat hooks, the kitchen table, the window.

Two rules make a route reliable:

1. **Always walk it the same way.** The order of the places is the order of the pairs.
2. **Use the same route for the same thing.** For example, one route for edges and another for corners, so the two memos never mix.

## Put images in places

Here's an edge memo to practise with.

<TraceWalk scramble="L2 U F L2" pieces="edges" mode="show" shows="no-breaks" label="Tracing edges for a memo to place along a route" />

The pairs are AX, CH and UP. Say your images are an axe, a chair and a hot-air balloon going up. Then:

- the **axe** is stuck in the front door;
- the **chair** is standing on the doormat;
- a **balloon** is tangled in the coat hooks.

Make each image *do* something to its place: stuck in it, standing on it, tangled in it. The interaction is what makes it stick.

## Read it back

Blindfolded, walk the route. At the door there's an axe: AX. On the mat, a chair: CH. In the hooks, a balloon: UP. The route hands you the order; the images hand you the letters.

<Note>Many solvers remember one piece type as images and the other as sounds, saying the letter pairs over in their head. You'll meet that choice in the next lesson. Start with images for both; it's the easiest to check.</Note>

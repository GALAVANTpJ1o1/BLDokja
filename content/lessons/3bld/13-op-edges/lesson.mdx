Edges work the same way as corners: one swap, and setups that bring each target to the swap spot. Only the pieces, the swap and the allowed moves change.

## The swap

The edge swap exchanges the buffer, <Buffer method="op" pieces="edges" />, with the swap spot, the top-left edge's top sticker: UL, <Letter sticker="UL" /> in your scheme. Its side effect swaps two top-layer corners, UBR and UFR.

<SwapAlg pieces="edges" />

It's 14 moves, and if you've learnt last-layer algorithms before, you may recognise it as the T permutation. A target already on the swap spot needs no setup:

## Setups

Setups for edges may use **D, L, Dw and Lw**: the bottom face, the left face, and the wide versions of both. Dw turns the bottom face with the E layer; Lw turns the left face with the M layer. That's what lets an edge from the middle layer or the far side of the cube reach the swap spot without touching the top-right.

Most edge targets take two or three moves; a few take one, and one takes four. Some examples:

The usual pattern: get the target into the left face with D or Dw moves, then turn the left face (L or Lw) so the target lands in the top-left on the top sticker.

## What's forbidden, and why

The swap needs the buffer UR and the corners UBR and UFR left where they are. So:

- **U and R** (and their wide versions) move the buffer and both corners.
- **F** moves UFR; **B** moves UBR.

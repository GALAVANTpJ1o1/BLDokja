Blindfolded methods are written as sequences of moves, and some of them use moves a normal solve rarely needs. This is a quick refresher, with a cube to try each one on.

## Face turns

A letter on its own turns that face a quarter turn **clockwise, as if you were looking straight at that face**:

- **R** turns the right face, **L** the left, **U** the top, **D** the bottom, **F** the front, **B** the back.
- An apostrophe, **R'** (said "R prime"), turns it counterclockwise.
- A 2, **R2**, turns it twice. Direction doesn't matter for a double turn.

The "looking at that face" part catches people out with L, D and B. L clockwise, seen from the left, moves the front of the left face down. Try R and L one after the other and watch them turn in opposite directions from the front.

## Slice moves

A slice move turns only a middle layer. There are three, and each one turns **the same way as one of the faces next to it**:

- **M** is the layer between L and R. It turns like **L**.
- **E** is the layer between U and D. It turns like **D**.
- **S** is the layer between F and B. It turns like **F**.

Slice moves shift the centres, so after an M the white centre isn't on top any more. That's expected. Blindfolded methods that use slices always put the centres back.

<MoveExplorer moves="M M' M2 E E' S S'" label="Slice moves: press a move to turn a middle layer" />

## Wide moves and rotations

A **wide move** turns a face together with the middle layer next to it. **Rw** turns the right face and the M layer together, in R's direction. It's also written with a lowercase letter, **r**; this site uses Rw.

A **rotation** turns the whole cube in your hands and changes nothing about the pieces:

- **x** turns the whole cube like R.
- **y** turns it like U.
- **z** turns it like F.

<MoveExplorer moves="Rw Rw' Uw x x' y y' z" label="Wide moves and rotations: press a move" />

<Note>For the rest of the path you'll mostly meet face turns, a few wide moves in setups, and M later on for edges. If a sequence looks strange, come back here and press the moves one at a time.</Note>

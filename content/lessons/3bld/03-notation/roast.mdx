Blindfolded methods are written as move sequences, and some of the moves are ones your normal solve has been avoiding for years. Refresher time. There's a cube to press, so you can't claim you didn't see it.

## Face turns

A letter on its own turns that face a quarter turn **clockwise, as if you were looking straight at that face**:

- **R** turns the right face, **L** the left, **U** the top, **D** the bottom, **F** the front, **B** the back.
- An apostrophe, **R'** (said "R prime", not "R tick"), turns it counterclockwise.
- A 2, **R2**, turns it twice. Direction doesn't matter for a double turn, so that's one thing you can't get wrong.

The "looking at that face" part is where people fall apart with L, D and B. L clockwise, seen from the left, moves the front of the left face down. Try R, then L, and watch them go opposite ways from the front. Yes, you've been doing L' when you meant L.

## Slice moves

A slice move turns only a middle layer. There are three, and each turns **the same way as one of the faces next to it**:

- **M** is the layer between L and R. It turns like **L**.
- **E** is the layer between U and D. It turns like **D**.
- **S** is the layer between F and B. It turns like **F**.

Slice moves shift the centres, so after an M the white centre isn't on top any more. That's intended, not you breaking it. Methods that use slices always put the centres back.

<MoveExplorer moves="M M' M2 E E' S S'" label="Slice moves: press a move to turn a middle layer" />

## Wide moves and rotations

A **wide move** turns a face together with the middle layer next to it. **Rw** turns the right face and the M layer together, in R's direction. It's also written lowercase, **r**. This site uses Rw, so no, you can't argue about it.

A **rotation** turns the whole cube in your hands and changes nothing about the pieces, which is why rotating the cube in frustration has never solved it:

- **x** turns the whole cube like R.
- **y** turns it like U.
- **z** turns it like F.

<MoveExplorer moves="Rw Rw' Uw x x' y y' z" label="Wide moves and rotations: press a move" />

<Note>For the rest of the path it's mostly face turns, a few wide moves in setups, and M later for edges. If a sequence looks strange, come back and press it one move at a time instead of guessing.</Note>

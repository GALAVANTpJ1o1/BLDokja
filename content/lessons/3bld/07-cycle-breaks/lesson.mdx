Sometimes the buffer's own piece comes home while other pieces are still out of place. Your chain of targets has run out, but the cube isn't solved. That's a **cycle break**, and it's completely normal: most scrambles have at least one.

## What's happening

The pieces of a scrambled cube form separate loops. The buffer is in one loop. Tracing from the buffer only ever walks around that loop; it can't reach pieces in another one. When that loop closes, you have to jump into the next loop yourself.

## How to break in

When the sticker you'd read next belongs to the buffer piece:

1. **Skip every solved piece.** A piece already in place with the right orientation needs no targets at all.
2. **Pick a sticker on a piece that isn't solved.** This site picks the one with the **lowest letter**, which gives you a rule instead of a decision. Write that letter down as your next target. It's a target you chose, not one you read.
3. **Carry on tracing** from there, exactly as before: look at the place you just named, read the sticker sitting in it.
4. The new loop **closes** when you read a sticker belonging to the piece you broke into. Write that letter down too. It's often a different sticker of that piece than the one you broke in on.

Then check again: is anything else unsolved? If so, break again.

In 3-style, every letter pair in your corner memo is one commutator. The buffer, the first target and the second target are the three pieces it cycles: the buffer's piece goes to the first target, the first target's piece goes to the second, and the second's comes back to the buffer.

## The buffer

This site's 3-style corners use the buffer **UFR**. That's not Old Pochmann's UBL: 3-style solvers pick a buffer that gives comfortable comms, and UFR is a common choice.

## A case is a pair

Tracing works exactly as before. The only change is what you do with each pair: one comm instead of two Old Pochmann swaps.

## Learning 378 cases

Nobody learns them in one go. A route that works:

1. **Start with pure commutators**, the cases with no setup. They teach the insertion and interchange shapes you'll reuse everywhere.
2. **Then one setup move**, grouped by the setup.
3. **Learn a case and its inverse together.** The case with its two targets the other way round is solved by the same comm with its two parts swapped.

The 3-style trainer shows every case with its comm, its moves and its inverse, and drills them with spaced review.

## Odd counts and twists

An odd number of corner targets leaves one target without a partner, and twisted corners need their own algs. Both have verified algs in this site's datasets; learn them after the cycles.

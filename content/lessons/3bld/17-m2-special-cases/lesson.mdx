UF and DB are the two edges M2 swaps besides the buffer and UB. A setup can't bring either to UB and still leave them home for the swap, so each has its own alg, one per sticker: **UF, FU, DB and BD**.

## The four algs

Each one does what a setup, M2 and undo would do if that were possible: the buffer's piece goes to the target, and the slice ends in the same state as after a normal M2.

Learn them in pairs: UF with DB, and FU with BD. Each pair belongs together because of the rule below.

## The odd/even rule

Every M2 turns the slice a half turn. After an odd number of them, the slice is still out: the piece that belongs at UF is sitting at DB, and the piece that belongs at DB is sitting at UF.

So when one of these four targets is the **second letter of a pair**, an odd number of M2s has just been done, and you shoot it as its partner:

- UF as the second letter: do **DB**'s alg;
- DB as the second letter: do **UF**'s alg;
- FU as the second letter: do **BD**'s alg;
- BD as the second letter: do **FU**'s alg.

As the first letter of a pair, each one is shot with its own alg.

<Note>The rule only depends on the position in the memo, not on what came before. Count pairs as you memorise and it's automatic: first letter, own alg; second letter, partner's.</Note>

The M2/OP trainer has a special-case drill that asks each of the eight cases, four targets in two positions, until they stick.

On a 3x3, corners and edges are always odd together, and one parity step handles both. A 4x4 has three piece types, and each one gets its own check and its own fix.

## Three separate counts

Count each memo as you make it. For each piece type, an odd count means that phase ends with its parity step; an even count means it doesn't. The three counts don't have to agree.

Why don't wings and corners go together, the way edges and corners do on a 3x3? Because different turns change them. An outer quarter turn like R moves four corners round a loop, which takes an odd number of swaps to undo, but it moves the wings in two loops, which is even. An inner quarter turn like 2R does the opposite: an odd loop of wings and no corners at all. A scramble mixes both kinds of turn, so each count comes out odd or even on its own.

## Centres: one more U2

X-centres don't have a parity of their own. Four of each colour are identical, so the count depends on which slots you choose while tracing. What the step fixes is the U layer the swaps left behind.

## Putting it together

For each piece type, in solve order: shoot every target, then check the count you memorised. Odd means its parity step, right there, before the next phase starts.

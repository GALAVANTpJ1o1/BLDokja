A 3x3 memo is about 20 letters. A 4BLD memo is well over twice that, and it's the part of 4BLD that takes the most practice.

## The numbers

On a typical scramble, holding the cube as scrambled:

- **x-centres:** about 19 targets;
- **wings:** about 24 targets;
- **corners:** about 8 targets.

That's around 51 letters, or about 26 pairs. With U2, r2 and Old Pochmann, executing them takes about 530 moves, so a first 4BLD solve is long. That's fine: nothing about it is fast yet, and none of it needs to be.

## Holding it

The letter pairs and images you built for 3BLD carry straight over; the only new thing is volume. Some habits that help:

- **Keep the lists apart.** Three separate stories, one per piece type, are easier to recall in order than one long one.
- **Use your palace.** A 4BLD memo needs more locations than a 3BLD one. Extend your route before you need it, not during a solve.
- **Review before the blindfold.** Run through all three lists once, in the order you'll execute them.
- **Note parity as you go.** When a list ends, say to yourself whether its count was odd.

## X-centres take the most thought

Wings and corners trace like 3x3 pieces: one right letter at each step. X-centres give you a choice, and a trace that makes good choices is shorter. When the buffer holds a colour, prefer a slot that doesn't already hold the buffer's own colour: that keeps the buffer's colour from coming back to it early, which saves cycle breaks.

Everything you learned for 3BLD still holds on a 4x4: memo from a buffer, shoot targets one at a time, count for parity. What changes is the pieces. This track only teaches the differences.

## Three piece types

A 4x4 has **8 corners**, exactly like a 3x3. They behave the same way too, and you'll solve them with the Old Pochmann you already know.

Where a 3x3 has one edge between two faces, a 4x4 has two pieces side by side. Each of these is a **wing**, and there are **24** of them. The two wings on an edge look like a pair, but they're different pieces: each one is the mirror image of the other, so a wing can only ever sit in its slot one way round. A wing can't be flipped.

Each face has four centre stickers, and each of those is its own piece, an **x-centre**. There are **24**. The four x-centres of one colour are identical, so it never matters which white x-centre ends up in which white slot.

<Cube puzzle="4x4x4" highlight="UFr FUr UFl FUl Ubl Ubr Ufl Ufr" label="A solved 4x4 with the two wings of the UF edge and the U face's x-centres lit" />

The lit stickers above are the two wings between the white and green faces, and the four white x-centres.

## No fixed centres

On a 3x3 the centres never move relative to each other, so the white centre tells you where white goes. On a 4x4 every centre piece moves. Turn an inner slice and x-centres travel from face to face; nothing stays put to say which face is which. So before you memorise, you have to decide how you're holding the cube. That's the next lesson.

## Inner slices and wide turns

A 4x4 has two layers between opposite faces. This site writes the inner layer next to R as **2R**: the slice one step in from R, turned the way R turns. Many tutorials write the same move as a lower-case **r**. A **wide** turn, **Rw**, turns R and 2R together.

<Cube puzzle="4x4x4" alg="2R" controls="true" label="The inner slice 2R on a solved 4x4" />

Play it and watch what moves: four wings go round the slice, and so do eight x-centres. No corner moves. That's the whole reason 4BLD has a wing method based on inner-slice turns.

<Note>When a tutorial you read elsewhere uses r, u or d for an inner slice, read it as 2R, 2U or 2D here. If it means a wide turn, it's Rw here.</Note>
